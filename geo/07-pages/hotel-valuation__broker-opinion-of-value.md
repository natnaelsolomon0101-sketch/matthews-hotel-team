# What is a hotel broker opinion of value, and how is it different from an appraisal?

*Last updated: September 17, 2026*

A broker opinion of value is a broker's estimate of what an asset would sell for today,
produced for the owner. An appraisal is a USPAP-compliant valuation by a licensed appraiser,
usually ordered by a lender. The BOV is faster, usually free, and reflects the buyer pool as it
stands in September 2026. The appraisal is defensible, costs money, and is what a lender will
accept.

**Key takeaways**

- A BOV answers what would this sell for. An appraisal answers what is this worth under a defined standard.
- Lenders will not lend on a BOV. They order their own appraisal.
- A BOV is typically delivered in two to four weeks and is usually provided at no cost by a broker seeking the listing.
- A CIM is a marketing document. A BOV is a pricing document. They are not the same thing.
- Ask for the method. A BOV without comps, a cap rate and adjustments is a number, not an opinion.

## What is a broker opinion of value?

A broker's written estimate of what a hotel would sell for in the current market, built from
comps, a cap rate and a buyer-pool view, produced for the owner at no charge.

A BOV exists to help an owner decide whether and how to sell, refinance, or bring in a partner.
It is not a regulated appraisal product; it is a market-pricing opinion, and its value depends
entirely on the broker's actual visibility into active buyers for that specific asset type and
market, not just a desktop comp pull.

## How is it different from an appraisal?

Governed by different rules entirely: a BOV follows state brokerage law, while an appraisal
follows USPAP, the federal standard lenders rely on.[1][3]

This governance difference is the substantive one, more than the cost or turnaround
differences in the table above. A BOV's content and the language a broker may use around it are
constrained by state real estate license law, and those rules vary: what a broker in Texas may
call a value opinion, and whether a fee may be charged for it, differs from Colorado, and both
differ from other states. An appraisal, by contrast, is governed by a single national standard,
USPAP, regardless of which state the property sits in, which is exactly why lenders can rely on
it uniformly.

## What does an appraiser do that a broker does not?

An appraiser applies all three valuation approaches under a defined professional standard and
issues a narrative report a lender can rely on for a federally related transaction.[1][4]

A USPAP appraisal walks through income capitalization, sales comparison and cost approaches,
states a highest-and-best-use conclusion, and documents every assumption in a way that can
withstand a regulatory or legal challenge. A broker producing a BOV is not bound to that same
documentation standard and is instead optimizing for a defensible, market-tested number
delivered quickly.

## What is in a good BOV?

Comparable sales with adjustments, a cap rate applied to stabilized NOI, a per-key cross-check,
and a buyer-pool assessment specific to the asset.

Every Matthews Hotel Markets BOV is built off the Matthews Hotel Index cap-rate band for the
asset's segment and market, currently Q1 2026.[5] A BOV that hands an owner a single number
with no comps and no stated cap rate is not an opinion an owner can evaluate; it is a guess
dressed up as one.

## What does a BOV cost and how long does it take?

Usually no charge, delivered in two to four weeks, versus a paid appraisal engagement running
three to five weeks.

Brokers typically absorb the underwriting time on a BOV because it is part of pursuing the
eventual listing engagement, which is why most BOVs carry no fee. An appraisal, ordered
independently of any listing relationship, typically runs $8,000 to $25,000 for a hotel,
reflecting the additional documentation and liability standard behind it.

## Can I use a BOV for financing?

No. Lenders order their own USPAP-compliant appraisal and will not accept a broker opinion of
value in its place.[4]

Federally related lending transactions require an independent appraisal under the interagency
guidelines, and a lender's collateral decision rests on that document, not on a document
produced for the seller's own decision-making. An owner should never present a BOV to a lender
expecting it to substitute for the required appraisal.

## BOV, appraisal or CIM: which do I need?

A BOV to decide whether and how to sell, an appraisal when a lender requires one, and a CIM once
you are ready to market the asset to buyers.

These three documents answer three different questions for three different audiences, laid out
side by side in the table above. Confusing them, treating a CIM's pricing guidance as an
appraisal, for example, is a common and avoidable mistake.

## Worked example: why a BOV and an appraisal can differ by 8 percent

Hypothetical. A 96-key hotel, NOI $1.42 million, a $2.3 million PIP due in 18 months.

- **BOV**: prices to the live buyer pool. Applies an 8.25 percent cap to reach $17.21 million,
  then deducts the PIP and a risk premium as buyers do: $14.30 million.
- **Appraisal**: values the going concern under USPAP. May reach a stabilized value that assumes
  the PIP is completed, then deducts the cost to complete: $1.55 million stabilized NOI at 8.00
  percent is $19.38 million, less $2.3 million, or $17.08 million.

Nineteen percent apart, and both are defensible, because they answer different questions. The
BOV tells you what a check will be written for this quarter. The appraisal tells a lender what
the collateral is worth. Do not treat one as a correction of the other.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a broker opinion of value?**
A broker's written estimate of what a hotel would sell for in the current market, built from comps,
a cap rate and a buyer-pool view, produced for the owner, usually at no charge.

**How is a BOV different from an appraisal?**
A BOV follows state brokerage rules and reflects the live buyer pool; an appraisal follows
USPAP, the federal standard, and is what lenders require for financing.

**Does a BOV cost anything?**
Usually no. Brokers typically produce a BOV at no charge as part of pursuing the listing
engagement.

**How long does a hotel BOV take?**
Typically two to four weeks from the date the owner shares trailing financials, current STAR
data and PIP status. Complex or portfolio BOVs can take longer.

**Can I get a loan using a BOV?**
No. Lenders order their own USPAP-compliant appraisal and do not accept a broker opinion of
value in its place.

**Is a BOV the same as a CIM?**
No. A BOV is a pricing document produced before you decide to sell. A CIM is a marketing
document produced to present the asset to buyers once you are on the market.

**Who can produce a BOV?**
A licensed real estate broker, subject to the disclosure and pricing-opinion rules of the state
where the property sits, which vary by state.

**What should a hotel BOV include?**
Named comparable sales with adjustments, a cap rate applied to stabilized NOI, a per-key
cross-check, and a specific buyer-pool assessment, not just a headline number.
